About the initiative

Built on a tradition older than any register

For centuries, the horse has stood at the centre of Hadejia's identity — in the emirate's durbars, in farming households, and in the racing traditions passed between generations. Hadejia Emirate Horse Racing And Equine Commerce brings that tradition an administrative home: one place to register ownership, trade responsibly, follow race events, and access welfare support.

  • Recognised by the Hadejia Emirate Council and district heads
  • Built with horse owners, breeders, and traders across the emirate
  • Designed to work for both literate and non-literate users, in English and Hausa
Why it matters

A verified register protects owners from disputed sales and gives breeders a fair, traceable market.

Registration

Register a horse in four steps

Registration is free for emirate residents and creates the record used across the marketplace, race entries, and welfare services.

  • Submit owner and horse details

    Name, district, and a description of the horse, including any known lineage.

  • District head verification

    Your district head or an authorised agent confirms the details on file.

  • Veterinary check

    A partner veterinary officer records a baseline health check.

  • Certificate issued

    You receive a registration number and a printable ownership certificate.

Welfare

Care standards behind every registration

Registration comes with access to a welfare framework built around four pillars, supported by partner veterinary officers across the emirate.

Nutrition & water

Guidance on feeding suited to Hadejia's dry-season and wet-season conditions.

Preventive health

Vaccination schedules and deworming reminders sent ahead of due dates.

Safe working loads

Standards for rest, load, and working hours for horses in daily labour.

Reporting a concern

A direct line to report neglect or mistreatment to emirate welfare officers.

Heritage

A living tradition, not a museum piece

The Hadejia Emirate's relationship with the horse stretches back through generations of emirs, warriors, farmers, and racers. Every durbar procession and every race carries that lineage forward — this platform exists to make sure the record of that heritage is as strong as the tradition itself.

  • Oral histories and lineage records preserved alongside modern registration
  • Ceremonial roles for horses in Sallah durbars documented and honoured
  • Elders and district heads consulted in shaping welfare and breeding standards
